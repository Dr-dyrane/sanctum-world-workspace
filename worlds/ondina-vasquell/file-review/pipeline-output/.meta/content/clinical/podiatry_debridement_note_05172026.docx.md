**PROCEDURE PERFORMED**

Bedside sharp debridement of devitalized soft tissue and fibrinous slough, left plantar forefoot (1st–2nd metatarsal head), with acquisition of deep soft tissue specimens for microbiology and surgical pathology.

**INDICATION**

Limb-threatening left diabetic foot infection in an insulin-dependent diabetic with peripheral neuropathy and peripheral arterial disease. Bedside source control is required for devitalized tissue and to obtain a deep tissue specimen for culture-directed therapy
. Imaging and clinical exam to date do not establish osteomyelitis; the goal of this procedure is soft-tissue source control and tissue acquisition, not bone resection.

**CONSENT**

Risks, benefits, and alternatives — including bleeding, infection progression, need for further debridement, possible operative debridement, and the limb-threat context — were reviewed with the patient in Spanish via the certified interpreter. The patient verbalized understanding and consented to bedside sharp debridement and deep tissue sampling. Daughter (Marisela Vasquell) was notified by phone prior to the procedure.

**ANESTHESIA**

Local infiltration with lidocaine 1% with epinephrine to the wound periphery; adequate anesthesia confirmed prior to incision. No sedation administered.

**TECHNIQUE**

The left plantar forefoot was identified, marked, and prepped with chlorhexidine in standard sterile fashion. A timeout was performed at the bedside with bedside nursing prior to incision. After local infiltration as above, devitalized soft tissue and fibrinous slough overlying the ulcer at the 1st–2nd metatarsal head were sharply excised using a #15 blade and curette down to viable, bleeding margins. The wound base was probed with a sterile blunt probe in a systematic fashion across the floor and undermined edges. The wound was copiously irrigated with sterile normal saline. Hemostasis was achieved with focal pressure; no electrocautery required. A moist saline-gauze dressing was applied with a dry secondary cover and the forefoot was placed in an offloading device.

Deep soft tissue specimens were obtained from the debrided base — separate samples for microbiology and for surgical pathology — and were handed off to the bedside RN with chain-of-custody documented.
