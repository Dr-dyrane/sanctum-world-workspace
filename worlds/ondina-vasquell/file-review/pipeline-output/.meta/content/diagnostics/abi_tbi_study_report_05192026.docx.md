## Indication

Limb-threatening left diabetic foot infection; evaluate perfusion adequacy.

## Technique

Bilateral lower-extremity arterial physiologic study performed at rest in the supine position in a temperature-controlled vascular laboratory. The protocol included:

- Brachial systolic pressures obtained bilaterally; the higher brachial pressure used as the denominator for index calculations.
- Segmental limb pressures measured at the high thigh, low thigh, calf, and ankle (posterior tibial and dorsalis pedis) bilaterally with appropriately sized pneumatic cuffs and continuous-wave Doppler signal acquisition.
- Ankle-brachial indices (ABI) calculated from the higher of the posterior tibial or dorsalis pedis systolic pressures at each ankle, divided by the higher brachial pressure.
- Doppler arterial waveform analysis recorded at the ankle level bilaterally.
- Photoplethysmographic (PPG) toe waveforms acquired from the great toes bilaterally with a digit cuff and digital PPG sensor.
- Toe systolic pressures measured by PPG with a digit cuff, and toe-brachial indices (TBI) calculated as the toe systolic pressure divided by the higher brachial systolic pressure.

The patient tolerated the examination. A standard left forefoot dressing was in place over the plantar wound; the toe sensor was applied to the contralateral first digit when ipsilateral application was limited by dressing position, and to the ipsilateral hallux on the right per protocol. Tracings of acceptable technical quality were obtained for the reported measurements.

Segmental pressures at the high thigh, low thigh, calf, and ankle were obtained bilaterally; ankle-level cuff pressures did not occlude the underlying tibial vessels at inflations up to the limit of the protocol, yielding indices above 1.3 bilaterally and rendering the ankle-brachial index uninterpretable as a perfusion measure on both sides. Ankle Doppler arterial waveforms were monophasic on the right and on the left. Photoplethysmographic toe waveforms were obtained from the great toes bilaterally and demonstrated dampened pulsatile contour on the left compared with the right; the corresponding toe systolic pressures and toe-brachial indices are tabulated above.

## Impression

1. Noncompressible ankle vessels bilaterally, consistent with medial arterial calcification. The ankle-brachial index is therefore not a reliable measure of lower-extremity arterial perfusion in this patient.
2. Toe-brachial indices and toe pressures are reduced, left more than right (left TBI 0.50, left toe pressure 55 mmHg; right TBI 0.84, right toe pressure 92 mmHg).
3. Ankle Doppler arterial waveforms are monophasic bilaterally.
4. Perfusion adequacy for wound healing is a clinical determination — formal correlation with vascular surgery is recommended.
