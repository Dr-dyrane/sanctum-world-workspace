## Failure Analysis

On trajectory 10 the model closed the outpatient vascular surgery referral. It deferred to the 05/23 triage addendum because that was the most recent vascular note, and it did so even after reading the perfusion study and quoting the reduced left toe pressures and the noncompressible ankle indices in the same response. The golden keeps the referral and rejects the addendum's closure. The model then folded the closure into the attending verification as a confirmed item, so a signing physician would see the vascular question as settled.

The error is clinically decisive. The ankle indices are noncompressible from medial calcification and the study itself calls them unreliable, so the toe pressures govern; at a toe-brachial index of 0.50 and a toe pressure of 55 the left foot has impaired perfusion. The signed vascular consult recommended outpatient follow-up and the attending's 05/21 note still had it being arranged, so the addendum was the lone outlier and the model sided with it. Closing the referral discharges a limb-threat diabetic foot to a skilled nursing facility with no vascular service following it and no trigger to reassess perfusion. If the wound stalls there is no one watching the circulation, which is the path to a nonhealing ulcer, tissue loss, and amputation. Deferring to a defensible-but-wrong authority over the chart's own evidence is the central failure.

## Grader Analysis

The 0.10 is appropriate even though the model completed the other referrals competently. The rubric is not an additive checklist; it caps the score on the vascular decision because closing the referral makes the coordination unsafe to sign. The grader set the output against the golden, found the referral closed on the addendum, and treated that as the deciding error. A note that discharges a limb-threat diabetic foot with no vascular follow-up is a failed deliverable, not a mostly-complete one, so the competent wound, antibiotic, primary care, and eye referrals do not lift it off the floor. Adding their weight toward a higher score would tell a signing physician the note is usable when it is not.

The catchers show this is calibration, not harshness. The runs that kept the referral and otherwise did the same competent work scored 0.82 to 0.88, so the grader rewards the complete coordination when the vascular call is right. The whole distance between this run and those catchers is the weight of that one safety-critical decision, which is what a level-of-care coordination should turn on. A deep floor for a polished note that misses a limb threat is correct, because the miss is the kind that defeats the document's purpose. The 0.10 is justified.
