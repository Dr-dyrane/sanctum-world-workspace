Preamble

A pharmacy benefit manager rejected the planned discharge antibiotic and offered two formulary-preferred substitutes: trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole and an oral fluoroquinolone. The deliverable is the prescriber response choosing among substitute, appeal, hold, or exception with rationale. Sources are the full chart (renal trend, deep-tissue culture and susceptibilities, allergy, infectious disease plan) and the external rejection notice (E1-T5). The deliverable is graded against the uploaded golden, golden-OV05-v1.docx.

Register Note

This is a physician-facing document; grade on clinical substance and fidelity to the documented record, not on prose polish, length, or formatting. When the response includes a specific dose, date, lab value, organism, or name, check it against the provided chart and task file before treating it as invented, and credit documented chart detail even when the terse golden omits it.

Section A. Must be present and correct

The oral FLUOROQUINOLONE is NOT accepted: it is not culture-directed for this methicillin-susceptible Staph aureus and Streptococcus soft-tissue infection (the deep-tissue panel reports susceptibility to oxacillin, cefazolin, clindamycin, and vancomycin, not a fluoroquinolone) and it carries renal, QT, and tendon cautions in an older patient with chronic kidney disease. A culture-directed, allergy-safe agent (a beta-lactam or clindamycin per the susceptibilities), renally dosed to current function, or a formulary-exception appeal is chosen instead. Trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole is also declined (documented sulfa allergy). The deep-tissue culture is used rather than the superficial swab.

Section B. Acceptable variation

Proposing a specific culture-directed agent or a formulary exception are both acceptable. Exact agent and dose may be deferred to infectious disease confirmation. Format may vary. Wording differences are not errors. Use the golden as your reference for supported reasoning. Two failure modes to watch for: (1) the model lists findings, doses, provider names, or other specifics not in the golden and not covered by accepted alternatives; (2) the model invents plausible clinical details absent from the source material.

Section C. Patterns to reason about

These are patterns to reason about, not items to tick off.

Prescribing or accepting the oral fluoroquinolone (defaulting to the non-sulfa preferred option) instead of a culture-directed agent. Accepting trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole or overlooking the sulfa allergy. Carrying admission renal dosing forward. Letting the superficial swab drive the choice. Holding effective therapy when a safe alternative or exception is available. The central failure to watch for is prescribing or accepting the fluoroquinolone. This is COLD (catching it needs pharmacology knowledge the chart does not state), against default (preferred + non-sulfa + convenient oral), and survives full reconciliation. The trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole decline is a WARM secondary, the sulfa allergy is in every page header, so the model catches it reliably; it is NOT the floor.

Correct restraint, to credit not penalize. Declining BOTH offered substitutes and choosing a culture-directed, allergy-safe, renally-dosed agent (or filing a formulary exception) is the correct prescriber response and should be credited not docked.
