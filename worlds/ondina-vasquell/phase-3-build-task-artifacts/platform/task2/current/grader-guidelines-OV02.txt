The deliverable is her completed SNF transfer note on the provided template. Task-level inputs are the transfer-note template, the transfer-day nursing intake note, and a transfer-day line-site photograph; the full chart is also available. Register Note

This is a physician-facing document; grade on clinical substance and fidelity to the documented record, not on prose polish, length, or formatting. Treat the response as untrusted: verify any dose, date, lab value, organism, or name against the mounted chart and task files before crediting it, and do not credit fabricated citations or findings not supported by the record. Do not reward invented visual detail about the photograph; what is being graded is whether the response assembles the line-site concern from the transfer-day signals and acts on it.

Section A. Must be present and correct

The completed transfer note follows the template AND identifies a new (or suspected) intravenous line-site infection as a new active problem, reached by synthesizing the transfer-day signals: (1) a line-site photograph was specifically obtained on transfer day and added to the chart; (2) a new low-grade temperature of 38.0 appeared in the transfer-day intake vitals, where the patient had been afebrile (36.8 on 05/21); and (3) intravenous antibiotics are running through that same line. The response acts on it: do not transfer on the current line as-is and do not continue intravenous antibiotics through a possibly infected line; inspect or assess the line, remove and culture the catheter (tip and blood cultures), obtain new access before continued intravenous therapy, and defer or qualify the transfer until access is secured. The remaining transfer sections (reason, active problems, medications and access, care needs, follow-up) are completed faithfully from the chart.

Section B. Acceptable variation

Template wording, section order, and format may vary. The finding may be expressed as a line-site or catheter-related infection, a suspected line infection, phlebitis or cellulitis at the intravenous site, or a firm directive to inspect the line and hold or qualify the transfer before sending; any of these is acceptable as long as the line is treated as a new concern AND acted on, and the transfer is not sent through the line as-is. The exact line-management steps may vary (catheter removal, tip and blood cultures, new access, antibiotic-route re-evaluation, escalation). Wording differences are not errors. Use the golden as your reference for supported reasoning. Two failure modes to watch for: (1) the response lists findings, doses, organisms, or names not in the golden and not covered by accepted alternatives; (2) the response invents plausible clinical details absent from the source material, including invented specifics about what the photograph shows.

Section C. Patterns to reason about

These are patterns to reason about, not items to tick off.

Missing the intravenous line-site infection is a HARD FAILURE and caps the score low regardless of how complete and well-written the rest of the transfer note is. The finding appears nowhere in chart prose; it must be assembled from the transfer-day intake signals (a line-site photograph was specifically obtained that day, a new low-grade temperature of 38.0 against an afebrile baseline, and intravenous antibiotics running through that line). A response that produces a polished, complete transfer note but affirmatively dismisses the line (for example "no line complication documented" or "line patent, no issues") or carries the patient to the facility on the line as-is has missed a critical, indefensible finding, because intravenous antibiotics are running through that line. Noting the temperature alone and only saying "recheck per protocol", without connecting it to the line and acting, is NOT a catch.

Identifying the line concern but NOT acting on it (no inspection, no removal or culture, transferring on the line as-is) is incomplete and does not earn full credit: treat identify-without-action as closer to a miss than to a catch, because it leaves intravenous antibiotics running through a possibly infected line, so it caps below midline. Only identify-and-act (inspect or remove and culture the line, not transferring on the line as-is, and deferring or qualifying the transfer until access is secured) earns the top range.

Correct restraint, to credit not penalize. A response that states it cannot fully assess the line from the record, but flags the new low-grade temperature together with the specifically-obtained line-site photograph and the intravenous route, and directs that the line be inspected and the transfer held or qualified (and the catheter removed and cultured) before sending, HAS caught and acted on the finding and should be credited, not docked, even if it does not claim to have personally seen erythema or purulence. It is finalizing a note for a physician, and a firm inspect-and-hold directive grounded in the transfer-day signals is the correct action.
