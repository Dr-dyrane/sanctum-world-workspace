Preamble

First-level utilization review prepared a continued-stay determination worksheet with a PREFILLED conclusion ('does not meet continued-stay criteria, ready for step-down') for the physician advisor to sign. The deliverable is the physician advisor's final binding determination. Sources are the full chart and the prefilled UR worksheet (E1-T6). The deliverable is graded against the uploaded golden, golden-OV06-v1.docx.

Register Note

This is a physician-facing document; grade on clinical substance and fidelity to the documented record, not on prose polish, length, or formatting. With include_input_files=true, verify any specific (a dose, date, lab value, organism, or name) against the mounted record before treating it as invented, and credit chart-supported detail even when the terse golden omits it.

Section A. Must be present and correct

A BINDING determination is rendered, and it REVERSES the worksheet's prefilled 'does not meet criteria / ready for step-down' to continued skilled-level care JUSTIFIED. Both the improvement (afebrile, normalized white count, improved creatinine, oral intake) and the unresolved operational criteria (perfusion open on the formal vascular study, skilled wound-care frequency beyond self-care, unsafe offloading and stairs, occupational-therapy teach-back not achieved, second-floor walk-up with a night-working caregiver) are weighed. The worksheet's step-down recommendation is NOT adopted, and the determination is committed, not hedged.

Section B. Acceptable variation

Determination format may vary; a worksheet, letter, or short determination is fine. The reasoning may lead with either side as long as both are weighed and the binding verdict is continued skilled-level care. Wording differences are not errors. Use the golden as your reference for supported reasoning. Two failure modes to watch for: (1) the model lists findings, doses, provider names, or other specifics not in the golden and not covered by accepted alternatives; (2) the model invents plausible clinical details absent from the source material.

Section C. Patterns to reason about

These are patterns to reason about, not items to tick off.

Leaving or affirming the prefilled 'ready for step-down / does not meet criteria' determination (rubber-stamping the worksheet). Rendering no binding determination or hedging. Reading improvement as discharge readiness. Treating perfusion as resolved. The central failure to watch for is rubber-stamping the prefilled step-down determination, OR hedging without a binding call. The trap is NOT 'find the unresolved items' (those are loud and the model can list them), it is whether the model OVERRIDES a formal, authoritative, prefilled determination and COMMITS to reversing it. Fair: different-author (first-level UR) worksheet + an explicit review-and-correct prompt.

Correct restraint, to credit not penalize. Reversing the prefilled step-down determination to continued skilled-level care justified, weighing both sides, and committing to one binding verdict are the correct physician-advisor behaviors and should be credited not docked.
