## Expected Senior-Clinician Output

The interdisciplinary care plan should be framed as hospitalist-led synthesis. It should not resolve the case by automatically deferring to Cardiology, Nephrology, Endocrinology, family, FI-W22, or the latest source. The expected output should explain that the plan must integrate objective trends, MAR actions, consultant recommendations, steroid-source hierarchy, family baseline knowledge, functional evidence, and discharge logistics.

For the cardiorenal medication issue, the output should state that both Cardiology and Nephrology are reasonable. Nephrology is appropriately concerned about recent AKI-on-CKD, potassium risk, low-normal blood pressure reserve, variable intake, volume status, and the risk of restarting renal/hemodynamic-sensitive drugs too quickly. Cardiology is appropriately concerned that HFrEF/CAD protective therapy should not be omitted indefinitely once the patient improves and objective trends support reassessment. The plan should therefore call for deliberate sequencing, explicit documentation of intentional holds or restarts, and monitoring/follow-up rather than a complete simultaneous restart or indefinite blanket hold.

The plan should use FI-W12 and FI-W13 carefully. The creatinine and potassium trends improved, supporting reassessment, but those trends do not independently settle restart timing. MAR actions show inpatient holds, continuations, and reassessments, but they are not a final discharge plan. The expected output should explicitly preserve that distinction.

For steroid and endocrine reasoning, the output should distinguish intended prednisone taper from actual recent adherence. Rheumatology documentation is the highest-authority outpatient source for PMR/prednisone taper intent. Verified medication reconciliation, pharmacy history, family report, and patient recollection add context but do not outrank rheumatology intent. Endocrinology's concern is clinically meaningful because chronic exposure and unclear taper/adherence create risk; the primary team is also reasonable because infection-oriented treatment and supportive care produced improvement. The plan should avoid abrupt steroid discontinuation and avoid declaring adrenal insufficiency as proven without sufficient evidence.

For discharge and function, the plan should integrate family, nursing, PT, OT, and case-management/social-work evidence. The patient is improved, but not cleanly back to baseline. Family concern should be incorporated because it reflects baseline knowledge. Therapy and nursing sources should inform supervision, assistive-device, medication-management, and education needs. Case management/social work should help determine whether services, equipment, transportation, family education, and follow-up logistics are feasible if discharge proceeds.

The care plan should treat FI-W22 as the visible planning snapshot that still requires reconciliation. It is useful for orientation but incomplete until medication reconciliation, family education, therapy/nursing input, services/follow-up logistics, and attending review are completed.
