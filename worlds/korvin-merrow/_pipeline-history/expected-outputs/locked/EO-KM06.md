## Expected Senior-Clinician Output

The patient-safety and readmission-risk review should identify risk signals that were visible before discharge planning closed. It should not invent whether Korvin was readmitted, whether he recovered, or whether an adverse event occurred. The expected output should be a physician-facing retrospective risk assessment grounded in the locked hospitalization record.

The review should start with the core safety frame: Korvin was medically improving but still operationally vulnerable. Objective infection, renal, potassium, hemodynamic, intake, and alertness trends improved. However, improvement did not automatically resolve medication-restart uncertainty, functional/cognitive recovery, family support, steroid-source ambiguity, or the risk of over-trusting the discharge-facing snapshot.

Medication-related risk signals should be prominent. Chronic HFrEF/CAD and diabetes medications were held, continued, or reassessed during acute illness. Nephrology and Cardiology had defensible concerns that needed synthesis. A safety review should identify the risk of either premature full restart without renal/potassium/BP/intake monitoring or accidental long-term omission of protective therapy. It should also identify the risk of treating MAR actions or FI-W22 language as a final discharge medication plan.

Steroid-source and endocrine-risk signals should be included but not overcalled. The chart showed chronic prednisone exposure, intended outpatient taper, inconsistent patient/family/bottle/refill information, and endocrinology concern for safe handling. The safety issue is whether source hierarchy and clear instructions were preserved, not whether adrenal insufficiency is retrospectively proven.

Functional and cognitive risk signals should be treated as distributed evidence. Nursing, PT, OT, family communication, and case management/social work sources showed that Korvin improved but remained below a clean independent baseline: cueing needs, fatigue, supervision/light assistance, ADL and medication-management vulnerability, and family concerns about first-days-at-home safety. A safety review should identify that these signals could be missed if the reviewer read only physician summaries or FI-W22.

The discharge-facing snapshot should be reviewed as a risk point. FI-W22 looked organized and reassuring, but it explicitly left final medication reconciliation, therapy/nursing review, education, support confirmation, and attending review incomplete. The expected safety review should identify the risk of treating this visible source as if it were a completed discharge plan.

The review should preserve family/team balance. Family concerns were specific and baseline-informed; the primary team also had a defensible reason to continue discharge planning given objective improvement. The safety question is whether the plan integrated both, not whether one side was obviously right.

## Expected Shape

A useful safety/readmission-risk review should include:

- visible pre-discharge risk signals;
- medication-restart and monitoring vulnerabilities;
- functional/cognitive and medication-management vulnerabilities;
- family/caregiver and support/logistics concerns;
- consultant-synthesis needs;
- source-hierarchy and prednisone-hierarchy risks;
- FI-W22 over-trust risk;
- concrete safety considerations without assigning blame or inventing outcomes.

## Prohibited Drift

The output should not create a readmission, non-readmission, adverse event, recovery course, RCA, blame narrative, final disposition judgment, scoring logic, or internal implementation labels. It should not create a standalone patient-risk-stratification workflow.
