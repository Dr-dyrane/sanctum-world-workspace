## Golden Reference

Use Golden-KM05 as the benchmark for a strong early follow-up assessment. The +7 date is a task anchor only. The golden should not permit invented follow-up facts.

## Clinical Reasoning That Should Be Present

A strong response should identify follow-up priorities based only on the locked hospitalization and discharge-risk substrate. It should not claim to know new symptoms, exam findings, vitals, labs, phone-call content, service starts, adherence, recovery, readmission, or outcomes at +7.

The response should reconstruct why Korvin is high risk after discharge planning: mixed-physiology hospitalization, AKI on CKD recovery, HFrEF/CAD medication complexity, diabetes therapy complexity, chronic prednisone/taper uncertainty, functional and cognitive vulnerability, medication-management risk, family-support questions, and an incomplete discharge-facing plan snapshot.

Medication follow-up should include verification of the actual home medication list, intentional holds/restarts, renal/cardiac monitoring needs, diabetes regimen safety, and whether inpatient correctional insulin was improperly carried forward.

Renal/cardiac safety should include attention to creatinine, potassium, blood pressure symptoms, volume status, intake, dizziness, dyspnea, edema, weight or congestion pattern, and follow-up feasibility when chart evidence supports those checks.

Steroid follow-up should focus on prednisone instruction clarity, rheumatology intent, endocrine-risk interpretation, family/patient understanding, and monitoring for clinically concerning symptoms without proving adrenal insufficiency or steroid causality.

Functional, cognitive, support, and medication-management follow-up should be grounded in nursing/PT/OT/family evidence and should ask whether home support is actually working.

## Scope And Format Expectations

The answer should read like an early follow-up priority assessment, not an actual completed clinic note with new observations. It can be organized by medication, renal/cardiac, steroid, diabetes, function/cognition, support/logistics, and escalation planning.

The response should be physician-facing and should explain what needs to be checked early because the hospital record left transition vulnerabilities.

## Good Practice That May Receive Credit But Should Not Be Required

- Stating explicitly that no new +7 facts are available.
- Including return precautions linked to the hospitalization risk substrate.
- Distinguishing intended discharge instructions from what must be verified in the home.
- Naming family/caregiver support as part of clinical safety.

Do not require the answer to name every possible monitoring item if it covers the major medication, renal/cardiac, steroid, diabetes, functional, cognitive, and support domains.

## Known Errors To Penalize

- Inventing +7 symptoms, vitals, labs, exam findings, visits, services, adherence, recovery, readmission, adverse event, or successful outcome.
- Treating the follow-up as retrospective certainty rather than transition-risk surveillance.
- Omitting medication reconciliation, renal/cardiac monitoring, steroid-plan clarification, diabetes safety, function/cognition, or support.
- Creating a final medication plan unsupported by the locked hospitalization evidence.
- Declaring adrenal insufficiency proven or sepsis resolved as the only relevant story.
- Treating FI-W22 as proof that discharge planning tasks were completed.
- Exposing internal implementation labels or scoring logic.

## Construction Notes For Future Scoring

Future scoring work should focus on whether the response identifies the correct follow-up priorities without inventing new post-discharge facts. This document assigns no points, thresholds, or pass/fail bands.
