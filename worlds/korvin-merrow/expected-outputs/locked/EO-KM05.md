## Expected Senior-Clinician Output

The early post-discharge follow-up assessment should reconstruct what needs close review at the +7 anchor based only on the hospitalization evidence. It should not pretend new follow-up findings exist. The expected output should frame the follow-up as a high-priority check on recovery trajectory, medication tolerance, renal/cardiac safety, steroid-plan coherence, function, cognition, home support, and whether discharge-planning caveats were translated into actionable support.

The assessment should summarize the hospitalization substrate: Korvin had a 3-week decline before admission with weakness, poor intake, near-fall/lightheadedness, confusion compared with baseline, possible urinary symptoms, medication-management mistakes, suspected infection physiology, AKI-on-CKD risk, HFrEF/CAD medication complexity, and chronic prednisone/taper uncertainty. During hospitalization he improved medically, but the discharge transition remained vulnerable because medication sequencing, functional reserve, steroid instructions, family support, and follow-up logistics required synthesis.

Medication follow-up priorities should be explicit. The output should identify that renal function, potassium, blood pressure symptoms, volume status, oral intake, and monitoring feasibility should guide whether HFrEF/CAD and diabetes medications were appropriately continued, held, restarted, or deferred. It should flag that early holds should not become accidental long-term omissions, but complete restart without monitoring could be unsafe. It should preserve that inpatient correctional insulin lispro is not a baseline outpatient medication.

Steroid follow-up priorities should focus on coherence and source hierarchy, not a hidden diagnosis. The output should identify the need to confirm what prednisone instructions were given, whether they align with rheumatology taper intent and inpatient endocrine concerns, and whether the patient/family understand them. It should not state that adrenal insufficiency occurred or that steroids were the only driver of symptoms.

Functional and cognitive follow-up priorities should be grounded in nursing/PT/OT/family evidence. The expected assessment should say the follow-up should check whether Korvin is approaching his baseline of independent basic activities with intermittent cane use and mild forgetfulness, whether he still needs supervision/cueing, whether medication-management support is actually in place, and whether family has enough clarity and capacity for the first days after discharge.

The assessment should discuss discharge-source hierarchy. FI-W22 was a useful pre-close plan snapshot but incomplete. The follow-up should therefore verify that medication education, final therapy recommendations, support plan, services/equipment, transportation, and follow-up scheduling were made actionable, without inventing whether they succeeded or failed.

## Expected Shape

A useful early follow-up assessment should include:

- hospitalization-risk substrate, not new +7 findings;
- medication monitoring and clarification priorities;
- renal/cardiac safety concerns to reassess;
- steroid-plan and prednisone-source coherence;
- functional, cognitive, ADL, and medication-management vulnerabilities;
- family/support and service-logistics follow-up needs;
- warning-sign and escalation considerations based on hospital evidence.

## Prohibited Drift

The output should not invent a post-discharge exam, vitals, labs, phone call, symptoms, recovery course, readmission, adverse event, or successful outcome. It should not create a golden answer, final medication plan, hidden diagnosis reveal, or internal implementation/scoring language.
