## Physician Response

Korvin is medically improved, and it is reasonable for the primary team to be actively discussing discharge planning. That is not the same as saying the chart, as of the pre-close snapshot, proves he is ready for discharge without further reconciliation.

Those facts make discharge planning plausible.

The counterweight is that his main residual risk is operational and functional, not simply "still sick." Before admission, he was slower but generally independent with basic self-care, used a cane intermittently, participated in household routine, and had family oversight for complex medications. During HD3-HD6, nursing, PT, OT, and family describe a patient who is improving but not clearly back to that baseline. Family concern should be taken seriously because it is baseline-specific rather than merely oppositional. Mara recognizes that Korvin is better than he was on arrival but worries that he remains less organized, more fatigued, and less reliable for medication timing than usual. That concern is consistent with nursing and therapy documentation. It does not automatically prove discharge is unsafe, but it must be reconciled before the plan is finalized.

The discharge-facing snapshot is reassuring but incomplete. It identifies the correct domains to finish: final medication reconciliation, family education, therapy/nursing input, assistive-device plan, supervision expectations, home services or outpatient therapy, transportation, follow-up, and final attending review. It does not itself complete those items or authorize discharge.

My assessment would be that discharge readiness is conditional. Home discharge could be defensible if, before discharge order entry, the team confirms an adequate supervision plan, reconciles PT/OT/nursing observations with the selected environment, teaches the final medication plan with family present, clarifies prednisone and high-risk renal/cardiac/diabetes medication instructions, arranges feasible services or follow-up, and documents the attending synthesis. Without those completed steps, the chart supports concern for premature discharge planning driven by medical improvement while functional, medication, and transition risks remain unresolved.

This is not a recommendation to ignore the primary team's improvement narrative or to treat family concern as dispositive. It is a recommendation to preserve the distinction between physiologic improvement and safe transition readiness.
