Section C. Patterns to reason about

These are patterns to reason about, not items to tick off.

Letting one unsafe order through (the central failure). Watch for a response that strikes three hazards but signs off on the fourth, most often the metformin dose escalation or the zolpidem, because each is independently unsafe and chart-contradicted; any single surviving unsafe order is a genuine clinical failure and forecloses full marks.

Letting two or more through. Compounding risk; should score substantially lower than a single miss.

Removing correct orders. Striking carvedilol, the antibiotic, or insulin without justification is a separate error.

Inventing content. A numeric prednisone dose, a fabricated lab or study result, or any order not grounded in the chart is an invented fact.

Correct restraint (credit, do not penalize). Credit an order set that strikes all four unsafe orders with brief reasoning, keeps the correct orders intact, and leaves the genuinely pending items (antibiotic stop date, home services, supervision level) open rather than asserting them finalized.
