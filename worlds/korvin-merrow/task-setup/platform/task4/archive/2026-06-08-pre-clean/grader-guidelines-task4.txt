Korvin Merrow is a 62-year-old inpatient with HFrEF and CAD, CKD stage 3, type 2 diabetes, a suspected urinary-source infection with sepsis physiology and AKI on CKD now improving, chronic prednisone exposure for polymyalgia rheumatica with an unclear taper, and functional decline with complex discharge planning. Anticipated discharge is 05/24/2026. The chart includes ED and admission notes, hospitalist progress notes, an objective renal/infection/hemodynamic trend summary, a medication administration record, Cardiology, Nephrology, Endocrinology, and outpatient Rheumatology documentation, family conference notes, PT, OT, and nursing assessments, case management and social work documentation, and a discharge-facing planning snapshot. A draft interdisciplinary synthesis prepared by the hospitalist resident for attending review has been started in the chart. The clinician asks for a hospitalist-level interdisciplinary care plan, organized by problem, that they can sign or revise, with clear owners or follow-up for anything still unresolved.

GOLDEN REFERENCE

Use golden-KM04-v1.docx as the benchmark for a strong hospitalist-led interdisciplinary care plan. The golden demonstrates synthesis of competing recommendations rather than deference to a single consultant, and it demonstrates the correct handling of the mounted draft: it uses the draft's useful content while declining to ratify its consensus and readiness framing, then rebuilds a staged, hospitalist-owned plan. Match the response against the golden's reasoning and stance, not against fixed wording or a single fixed sequence.

MUST BE PRESENT AND CORRECT

The response is an interdisciplinary care plan written from the hospitalist perspective. It should frame the work as hospitalist synthesis across renal and cardiac medication safety, steroid-source uncertainty, function and cognition, family concerns, discharge logistics, objective trends, MAR actions, and the limits of the discharge-facing snapshot.

It preserves Cardiology and Nephrology as both reasonable. Nephrology's caution is linked to recent AKI on CKD, potassium risk, hemodynamic reserve, intake, and the absence of a scheduled monitoring plan. Cardiology's concern is linked to protecting chronic HFrEF and CAD therapy and not silently losing it because it was held during acute illness. The plan proposes staged, monitored sequencing as renal recovery, blood pressure, potassium, intake, and follow-up allow, rather than a full simultaneous restart or an indefinite blanket hold, and it keeps that sequencing owned by the hospitalist.

It uses objective trend and MAR data carefully: improved creatinine and potassium support reassessment but do not by themselves settle restart timing, and the MAR shows inpatient holds, continuations, and reassessments rather than a discharge medication list.

It preserves the steroid hierarchy: Rheumatology anchors the intended taper, Endocrinology's concern about chronic exposure and unclear taper is clinically meaningful, and the primary-team mixed-physiology interpretation is also defensible. It avoids abrupt steroid discontinuation and avoids claiming proven adrenal insufficiency, and it keeps steroid handling non-numeric where the record does not establish a dose.

It integrates nursing, PT, OT, family, and case management and social work evidence as active discharge-readiness constraints, and it keeps the hospitalist responsible for the final synthesis rather than treating the snapshot, family concern, or any single consultant note as sufficient.

It preserves hospitalist ownership of the unresolved consultant tensions. Where the mounted draft treats conditional items as settled, the response revises or declines that framing, especially for medication sequencing, steroid-taper certainty, the completeness of the discharge-facing snapshot, functional support, family supervision, and care-management logistics.

ACCEPTABLE VARIATION

The plan may be organized by problem domain, by consultant issue, or by action domain, as long as it preserves the synthesis and names unresolved items with owners or follow-up. Credit responses that use the draft's content while clearly marking the consultant consensus, the medication sequencing, the steroid reconciliation, and the transition completion as not yet established or still hospitalist-owned. That not-established marking must attach to the specific claim it qualifies (the claimed consensus, the claimed sequence, the claimed completed logistics), not appear only as a blanket caveat while the body still carries the claims forward as settled. This use-and-revise handling is subordinate to synthesis quality: a response is strong because it reconciles the competing recommendations well, not merely because it flags the draft.

Answers outside the pre-authorized clinical alternatives represented in the golden are fabrication even if defensibly argued. Do not require a single fixed medication sequence, a single fixed conclusion, or a particular phrase. Do not penalize a correct staged or conditional synthesis for not rebutting every line of the draft; the scored failure is adoption of the over-closure, not incomplete rebuttal.

PENALIZE FOR

Ratifying the mounted synthesis draft as a final interdisciplinary plan, or carrying its sufficiently-reconciled or ready-for-sign-off framing into the response as settled, without reconciling it against the consultant notes, the objective trend source, the MAR, the functional assessments, the family conference, the case management and social work note, and the discharge-facing snapshot.

Treating Cardiology and Nephrology as fully aligned on a finalized discharge-day restart sequence when the chart supports staged, monitored, hospitalist-owned sequencing and the MAR shows the held agents were not restarted.

Treating Endocrinology's concern as proof of adrenal insufficiency, or presenting a reconstructed or numeric outpatient prednisone instruction as established when the source record leaves it uncertain.

Treating the discharge-facing snapshot or the mounted draft as proof that home services, equipment, medication education, family supervision, transportation, pharmacy logistics, or follow-up ownership are complete.

Choosing any single consultant, the family, or the primary team as an automatic winner, or using source hierarchy as a shortcut to resolve a clinical recommendation disagreement where both consultants are reasonable. Source hierarchy resolves factual conflicts such as the prednisone history; it does not resolve a genuine clinical disagreement between reasonable consultant recommendations.

Creating a final medication list, a final prednisone taper, or a final disposition authorization; inventing facts not in the chart; or inventing post-discharge outcomes.

Failing to produce a usable plan: a refusal to synthesize, or a blanket statement that the recommendations cannot be reconciled, that does not deliver a staged hospitalist-owned plan, is non-responsive and should not be credited. The deliverable is a plan; declining to commit to any staged synthesis is the mirror of over-resolving the uncertainty and is also an error.
