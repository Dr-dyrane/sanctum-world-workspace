Grader Guidelines - Hospital Discharge Summary (Korvin Merrow)

Task context

Korvin Merrow is a 62-year-old man admitted 05/18/2026 with suspected urinary-source infection and sepsis physiology on a background of HFrEF, CAD with remote PCI, CKD stage 3, type 2 diabetes, polymyalgia rheumatica on chronic prednisone of unclear outpatient taper and adherence, and additional chronic illness. Discharge was planned for 05/24/2026. The agent was asked to produce a discharge summary for the hospitalization, written as a clinical narrative that reflects how the course actually evolved rather than a copied-forward problem list. The agent works from the 26-document inpatient chart (ED and admission documentation, daily hospitalist notes, objective trend data, cardiology, nephrology, and endocrinology consultations with addenda, therapy and nursing assessments, family communication, case management, and a discharge-facing plan snapshot).

Golden reference

Use golden-response-task2-v1.docx as the benchmark for a strong discharge-summary synthesis. Treat it as a reference for the clinical story and coverage rather than a script. A response may use different wording, ordering, or emphasis and still score well if it faithfully summarizes the hospitalization and preserves the uncertainty the record preserves.

Must be present and correct

The summary must read as a clinical narrative of the hospital course, not a problem-list dump or a final order set. It should summarize the admission as a mixed-physiology hospitalization that began with a reasonable suspected urinary-source infection frame, and must not carry the early sepsis-only impression forward as the entire discharge narrative.

The hospital course should be organized by evolution. The early days reflect acute stabilization, infection-directed treatment, cautious renal and hemodynamic management, and medication holds or reassessment. The middle days reflect the emergence of functional and cognitive concerns, consultant input, and reconstruction of the prednisone history. The later days reflect medical improvement with unresolved transition questions.

Consultant chronology and medication uncertainty must be preserved without creating a final medication reconciliation. Cardiology and Nephrology should be described as having defensible but different emphases, and Endocrinology's steroid-risk concern should be described without proving a single endocrine diagnosis.

Prednisone should be summarized with the source hierarchy intact: rheumatology documentation is the strongest source for the intended outpatient taper; medication reconciliation and pharmacy data support chronic and recent exposure but do not prove actual ingestion; family report and patient recollection explain uncertainty but do not override stronger sources. The summary should not assert a definitive home prednisone adherence history the chart cannot support, and should not treat adrenal insufficiency as established.

Functional, cognitive, family, and discharge-planning evidence should be visible, integrating nursing, PT, OT, family, and care-management documentation that shows improvement without a clean return to baseline. The discharge-facing snapshot should be used as planning context, not as the completed summary, and unresolved follow-up needs (medication sequencing, renal/potassium/blood-pressure monitoring, steroid instruction clarity, functional support, family education, outpatient follow-up) should be preserved.

Acceptable variation

The summary may be organized by hospital course, by active problems, or by major clinical domains, as long as the evolution and the unresolved transition issues remain clear. No specific discharge-summary template is required if the answer is clinically faithful and complete. Some answers will also separate the initial working diagnosis from the final hospital-course synthesis, describe medical improvement and transition risk together, list active problems in a coherent order, or explain why family concern is baseline-informed rather than generic resistance. These are marks of a strong answer; their absence alone is not an error if the core synthesis is intact.

Use the golden as the reference for what the chart supports. Treat as a problem any clinical specific that contradicts the golden or asserts closure the record does not support, such as invented culture results, a final steroid diagnosis, a final medication plan, post-discharge symptoms or outcomes, or a completed discharge disposition.

Penalize for

Carrying the sepsis-only frame forward as the whole discharge narrative. Saying the infection was false, that steroids explain everything, or that adrenal insufficiency is established without support. Inventing culture results, a final steroid diagnosis, post-discharge symptoms, services, visits, readmission, or outcomes. Creating a final medication plan or final discharge disposition rather than a summary. Omitting consultant chronology. Omitting functional, cognitive, family, or discharge-readiness context. Treating the discharge-facing snapshot as the completed discharge summary.
