Korvin Merrow is a 62-year-old man with HFrEF, CAD with remote PCI and stent, CKD stage 3, type 2 diabetes, hypertension, hyperlipidemia, diabetic peripheral neuropathy, OSA, anemia of CKD, chronic-steroid-related osteoporosis, and polymyalgia rheumatica on long-term prednisone with an unclear recent taper. He was admitted 05/18/2026 and is anticipated for discharge 05/24/2026.

Reason for admission and initial working diagnosis. He presented after roughly three weeks of progressive weakness, poor oral intake, lightheadedness with a near-fall at home (no head strike, no loss of consciousness), and family-noted confusion, with possible urinary symptoms and recent medication-management errors. The initial working diagnosis was suspected urinary-source infection with sepsis physiology, a reasonable frame given the urinary symptoms, leukocytosis, hemodynamic vulnerability, and altered mentation. The admission picture was managed as mixed physiology rather than infection alone, with AKI on CKD, poor intake, polypharmacy, chronic steroid exposure, and deconditioning all contributing.

Hospital course. He was admitted to the hospitalist service under Dr. Elian Vossmere and started on infection-directed treatment with a cautious fluid strategy given HFrEF and CKD. Admission labs were notable for creatinine 2.62 mg/dL against a CKD baseline of about 1.6 to 1.8, potassium 5.1 mmol/L, and WBC 15.6 K/uL, with an earlier febrile pattern and hyperglycemia. Renal and hemodynamic-sensitive chronic medications, including sacubitril/valsartan, spironolactone, empagliflozin, metformin, and diuretic therapy, were held or cautiously managed during AKI, poor intake, and low-normal blood pressures, and carvedilol was managed in hemodynamic context. By HD2 he was less acutely ill with improved alertness and intake but remained weak and slower than his baseline; these early holds were acute-illness decisions, not a final chronic medication plan.

Over HD3 to HD4 the course was clearly multifactorial. Infection markers, renal function, potassium, and intake improved (creatinine 2.12 then 1.98, potassium 4.7 then 4.6, WBC 11.9 then 10.8), but nursing, physical therapy, and occupational therapy documented function and cognition below his described baseline, with limited ambulation tolerance, supervision for transfers, and difficulty with multi-step tasks and explaining his own medication routine. Consultants sharpened the discharge-relevant issues. Nephrology (Dr. Iven Solthar) emphasized sequencing the renal and hemodynamic-sensitive medications during AKI recovery rather than restarting them simultaneously, with particular caution on spironolactone and the sacubitril/valsartan pathway given potassium and blood-pressure risk. Cardiology (Dr. Maris Caldrane) emphasized not losing HFrEF and CAD protection to inertia, continuing aspirin and atorvastatin, and planning deliberate reintroduction rather than vague indefinite holds. The two services differed on timing and sequencing, not on whether renal safety mattered. Outpatient rheumatology provenance (Dr. Soren Halvek) became available and clarified an intended cautious taper; endocrinology (Dr. Nerea Veylorn) judged the chronic steroid exposure clinically relevant with a plausible risk of adrenal suppression but did not establish adrenal insufficiency, and recommended avoiding abrupt prednisone discontinuation while the source history was reconstructed.

By HD5 to HD6 he was medically improved: afebrile, creatinine near his CKD baseline at 1.86 then 1.80, potassium 4.4, WBC 9.4, with better intake and alertness, and discharge planning was reasonable to continue. Case management engaged on services, equipment, transportation, and follow-up logistics. Therapy and nursing still described limited endurance, supervision needs, and vulnerability with complex medication self-management, and the family, primarily Mara Merrow, remained concerned that better than admission was not the same as a return to baseline. The HD6 discharge-facing snapshot recorded an anticipated discharge of 05/24 pending final medication reconciliation, education, and transition supports; it was a planning snapshot, not a completed discharge order, final medication list, or final disposition.
