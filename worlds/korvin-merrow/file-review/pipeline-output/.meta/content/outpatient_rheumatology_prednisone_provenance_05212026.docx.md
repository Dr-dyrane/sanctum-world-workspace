### Source-of-truth implication

Within the outpatient prednisone source hierarchy, this rheumatology record reflects intended PMR management and should be weighted above patient recollection, family interpretation of bottle labels, and raw refill history when the question is what was *intended*. It is the highest-authority source for outpatient taper *intent* only.

It does not, however, establish several things that the inpatient team may be tempted to read into it:

- This practice **cannot verify actual home adherence** between visits. Whether Mr. Merrow took the intended taper, took an older taper instruction still visible on a prior label, took more than intended during a flare, or took less than intended during a period of decline is not something an outpatient note can answer.
- This note describes the **intended outpatient plan, not inpatient management**. Hospital steroid handling is a separate inpatient decision and is not directed by this document.
- This record does **not** establish that the patient's current weakness, lightheadedness, altered mentation, or hemodynamic vulnerability is primarily caused by adrenal suppression. Steroid exposure history is a risk substrate, not a diagnosis.
- This record does **not** establish that the inpatient team's early urinary-source sepsis frame is wrong. The two interpretations are not in competition through this document.
