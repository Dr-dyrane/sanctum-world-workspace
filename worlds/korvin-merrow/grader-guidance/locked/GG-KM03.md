## Golden Reference

Use Golden-KM03 as the benchmark for a strong discharge-readiness and care-coordination assessment. The golden should not become a discharge authorization key. Credit answers that distinguish physiologic improvement from transition readiness even when their final wording differs.

## Clinical Reasoning That Should Be Present

A strong response should acknowledge both sides of the discharge tension. Korvin is medically improved enough that discharge planning is reasonable to explore. That improvement does not prove the transition is safe or complete.

The answer should compare current status with baseline. Baseline function included relative independence with chronic illness, occasional cane use, mild forgetfulness, and family involvement in complex medication routines. Current evidence shows improvement but ongoing fatigue, cueing needs, mobility limitations, ADL vulnerability, medication-management difficulty, and family concern.

The response should integrate nursing, PT, OT, family communication, case management, social work, objective trend data, and FI-W22. It should not rely on FI-W22 alone. It should identify that FI-W22 lists reasonable planning items but does not prove completion, authorization, acceptance, or sufficiency.

Family concern should be treated as meaningful baseline evidence. It should not be treated as automatically dispositive. The primary team's improvement narrative should also remain defensible.

Medication complexity should be recognized as a discharge-readiness issue because renal/HF medication sequencing, prednisone instructions, diabetes plan, inpatient-only medication actions, and family-supported education all affect whether home transition is practical.

## Scope And Format Expectations

The response should be a physician-facing assessment of risks, gaps, and coherence issues in the discharge plan. It should not issue a final discharge order, approve services, or declare discharge definitively safe or unsafe.

Accept prose, structured bullets, or a domain-based assessment if it addresses medical stability, function/cognition, medication management, family support, services/equipment, transportation, follow-up, and remaining attending synthesis.

## Good Practice That May Receive Credit But Should Not Be Required

- Stating a conditional discharge-readiness conclusion.
- Separating resolved medical issues from unresolved operational issues.
- Naming specific domains that must be confirmed before discharge order entry.
- Identifying family education as a care-coordination requirement, not just a courtesy.

Do not require a single final conclusion if the response appropriately describes conditional readiness and unresolved items.

## Known Errors To Penalize

- Treating improved creatinine, potassium, intake, alertness, or fever burden as enough for discharge authorization.
- Treating family concern as either irrelevant or automatically controlling.
- Relying on FI-W22 as a completed discharge plan.
- Ignoring nursing, PT, OT, CM/SW, family, or medication-management evidence.
- Creating final service approvals, equipment authorization, transportation completion, home health start, or discharge order.
- Inventing post-discharge outcomes.
- Blaming the primary team or writing an RCA-style critique.

## Construction Notes For Future Scoring

Future scoring work should reward careful distinction between medical improvement and transition safety, plus integration of distributed functional and support evidence. This document assigns no points, thresholds, or pass/fail bands.
