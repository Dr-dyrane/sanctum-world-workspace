## Golden Reference

Use Golden-KM04 as the benchmark for a strong hospitalist-led interdisciplinary care plan. The golden demonstrates synthesis of competing recommendations, not deference to a single consultant.

## Clinical Reasoning That Should Be Present

A strong response should frame the task as hospitalist synthesis across renal/cardiac medication safety, steroid-source uncertainty, functional/cognitive status, family concerns, discharge logistics, objective trends, MAR actions, and FI-W22 limitations.

The answer should preserve Cardiology and Nephrology as both reasonable. Nephrology's caution should be linked to recent AKI on CKD, potassium risk, hemodynamics, intake, and monitoring. Cardiology's urgency should be linked to HFrEF/CAD protection and avoiding accidental long-term omission of chronic therapy. The answer should propose staged, monitored sequencing rather than full simultaneous restart or indefinite blanket hold.

The response should use objective trend and MAR data carefully. Improved creatinine and potassium support reassessment but do not settle restart timing. MAR actions show inpatient holds, continuations, and reassessments but are not a discharge medication list.

Steroid reasoning should preserve the prednisone hierarchy. Rheumatology anchors intended taper. Endocrinology's concern is clinically meaningful, while primary-team mixed-physiology interpretation also remains defensible. The answer should avoid abrupt steroid discontinuation and avoid claiming proven adrenal insufficiency.

The care plan should integrate nursing, PT, OT, family, CM/SW, and discharge logistics. The hospitalist remains responsible for final synthesis rather than treating FI-W22, family concern, or a consultant note as sufficient.

## Scope And Format Expectations

The response should be an interdisciplinary care plan. It may be organized by problem domain, consultant issue, or action domain if it preserves synthesis and unresolved items.

It should not create final consultant orders, a final medication list, a final prednisone taper, a final disposition authorization, or a complete discharge plan. It should identify plan concepts and reconciliation needs.

## Good Practice That May Receive Credit But Should Not Be Required

- Writing the plan explicitly from the hospitalist perspective.
- Naming what each consultant is right to worry about.
- Separating inpatient action evidence from outpatient plan decisions.
- Including family/caregiver communication and care-management logistics as part of the plan.

Do not require a single fixed care-plan sequence if the answer preserves the reasoning and safety constraints.

## Known Errors To Penalize

- Choosing Cardiology, Nephrology, Endocrinology, family, or primary team as automatic winner.
- Using source hierarchy as a shortcut to resolve clinical recommendation disagreement.
- Creating a final medication list, final prednisone taper, or final disposition authorization.
- Treating FI-W12 trends or FI-W13 MAR actions as final answers.
- Treating FI-W22 as complete.
- Ignoring family, nursing, PT, OT, CM/SW, steroid-source, or medication-management evidence.
- Over-resolving discharge uncertainty.
- Inventing facts not in the locked chart.

Future scoring work should evaluate synthesis quality, preservation of defensible disagreement, and avoidance of shortcut reasoning. This document assigns no points, thresholds, or pass/fail bands.
