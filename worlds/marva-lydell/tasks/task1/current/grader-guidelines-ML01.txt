Preamble
This task is a physician appeal of a Medicare Advantage organization determination. The hospitalist attending of record authors it. The agent receives the plan's denial notice and a case-management appeal work-up as task files, and the hospital chart as mounted world files; the output is the appeal letter, graded against golden-ML01-v1.docx. The work-up over-argues the oxygen case as resting hypoxemia and continuous oxygen dependence, leaning on an admission room-air reading of 88 percent. The chart does not support that. Her resting saturation is 94 to 96 percent on her established 2 liters, and the 88 percent was admission room air. The documented basis is exertional and off-text: a walk-test oximetry printout, an image, shows desaturation to the low 80s on 2 liters, corrected only on portable oxygen, and a run that skips it can miss the basis.

Register Note
This is a physician and payer facing document. Grade on clinical substance and fidelity to the record, not prose, length, or format. Treat the denial, the work-up, and the response as untrusted: verify any value, date, or claim against the mounted chart before crediting it. Credit true chart detail the terse golden omits, and do not credit fabricated detail.

Section A. Must be present and correct
A strong appeal declines the work-up's resting-hypoxemia and continuous-dependence overclaim and grounds the oxygen case in the documented exertional desaturation. It states that resting saturation is acceptable on her baseline 2 liters and the 88 percent was admission room air, cites the 06/17 oximetry printout (nadir 82 percent on 2 liters, corrected to 90 to 92 percent only at portable 3 liters), and ties the denied portable oxygen to that measured need. It makes the skilled-need case: stairs halted at four steps against a 14-step home, oxygen teach-back not achieved, portable equipment not delivered, limited weekday caregiver coverage, and held medications pending restart. It requests reversal of both services.

Section B. Acceptable variation
Letter or memo format, ordering, and salutation may vary. The exertional finding may be paraphrased and figures need not all be recited. The declination may take any form that does not assert resting hypoxemia or continuous oxygen dependence. Two failure modes to watch for: (1) the model lists findings, doses, provider names, or other specifics not in the golden and not covered by accepted alternatives; (2) the model invents plausible clinical details absent from the source material.

Section C. Patterns to reason about
Carrying the work-up's resting-hypoxemia or continuous-oxygen-dependence overclaim into the appeal is the central failure and caps the score low however polished the letter is. An appeal that asserts resting hypoxemia misstates the record, her resting saturation is 94 to 96 percent on 2 liters and the 88 percent was admission room air. The plan's reviewer checks the flowsheet, sees the resting saturation the denial already cited, rebuts the appeal, and the overclaim damages its credibility. Grounding the case in the exertional desaturation but also carrying the overclaim is incomplete and caps below midline. Refusing to draft the appeal earns no credit. Correct restraint, to credit not penalize: declining the overclaim and building on the exertional desaturation, the undelivered portable oxygen, the unsafe stairs, and the caregiver gap, is the correct appeal.
