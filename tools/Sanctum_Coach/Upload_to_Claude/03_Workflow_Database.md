AUTHORITATIVE LIST. Every task in a brainstorm MUST map to a workflow on this list. Unlike the template DataBank, you may NOT use a workflow that is not here. Focus on P0 and P1; P2 is acceptable but lower priority. At least one task per world must be a P0.

PHYSICIAN-VOICE RULE (from the official Guidance tab, verbatim intent):
Some workflows here are not obviously physician-specific. They are acceptable IF the workflow can be framed as a deliverable a physician either produces themselves OR reviews/supervises in a medical-director or healthcare-admin capacity. Example: 'Inpatient Pharmacotherapy Management' reads as pharmacist work, but it is acceptable if the task prompt frames the physician as IN COMMUNICATION or INTERACTING with the pharmacist and the final deliverable involves both inputs. The non-negotiable: the voice and framing must be from the physician's perspective. Sample framing: "Draft a response to the pharmacist explaining why vancomycin would be inappropriate for this patient, citing the latest IDSA guidelines as of 2025." So: do not reject a task just because its workflow sounds non-physician - check whether it can be framed in the physician's voice with physician input. Reject only when the deliverable is authored in a non-physician's voice with no physician production or supervision.

- Ambulatory Clinical Progress Note Documentation (SOAP Notes) :: Each progress note requires synthesizing information from multiple sources — patient verbal history (subjective), physical examination and diagnostic data (objective), clinical reasoning across active problem list and differential diagnoses (assessment), and treatment planning incorporating evidence-based guidelines, medication management, referrals, and follow-up (plan). The clinician must integrate real-time patient interview data with pre-existing EHR records, lab results, imaging, and prior notes into a coherent clinical narrative. - Charge Capture Review and Reconciliation :: Requires coordinating data from multiple clinical and financial systems—EHR encounter records, operating room logs, pharmacy dispensing systems, radiology information systems, laboratory information systems, supply chain/implant tracking, and the hospital chargemaster—to ensure all billable services are captured and correctly coded. The reconciliation process involves cross- referencing appointment schedules against charge postings, identifying discrepancies, investigating root causes with clinical departments, and resolving exceptions before claims submission. Memorial Hermann Health System implemented automated charge capture reconciliation through FinThrive after finding that manual charge auditing caused missed charges, billing errors, and revenue leakage. This multi-system orchestration distinguishes it from single-source adjudication tasks.
- Chronic Care Management Care Plan Development :: The clinician must orchestrate data from multiple sources (EHR, lab systems, pharmacy records, specialist notes, patient self-reports, community resource directories) to develop a comprehensive, individualized care plan. The care plan must address each qualifying chronic condition with problem-specific goals, interventions, responsible parties, and timelines. It requires cross-referencing medication lists for interactions and adherence barriers, coordinating with specialists and community services, incorporating patient preferences and self-management capabilities, and documenting the plan in the EHR in a format accessible to all providers involved in the patient's care. Monthly reviews require synthesizing interval changes (new lab results, hospitalizations, specialist visits, medication changes) and revising goals and interventions accordingly. The workflow spans clinical judgment, patient communication, care coordination, and regulatory documentation compliance.
- Claims Denial Analysis and Appeal Preparation :: The core challenge is adjudication — the denial analyst must weigh payer denial rationale (expressed via CARC/RARC codes) against clinical documentation, medical necessity criteria, payer contract terms, and regulatory requirements to determine whether a denial is valid or overturnable, then construct a persuasive clinical and regulatory argument for appeal. Each denial requires judgment about root cause classification, appealability, optimal appeal strategy, and strength of clinical evidence
- Clinical Documentation Improvement (CDI) Query Generation :: The CDI specialist must adjudicate whether clinical evidence in the medical record warrants a physician query versus accepting documentation as-is, determine the appropriate query type (open-ended, multiple-choice, yes/no), select which clinical indicators to cite without leading the physician toward a specific diagnosis, and assess whether the documentation gap has DRG or quality reporting implications. Each query balances documentation accuracy against compliance risk, clinical nuance, and physician relationship management. This is distinct from the downstream query response review and lifecycle management workflows.
- Clinical Documentation Improvement (CDI) Query Management :: The CDI specialist must adjudicate whether clinical evidence in the medical record warrants a physician query, determine the appropriate query type (open-ended, multiple-choice, yes/no), select relevant clinical indicators to cite without leading the physician, and decide when to escalate unanswered queries versus re-querying or accepting documentation as-is. Each query requires balancing documentation accuracy goals against compliance constraints, physician relationship management, and regulatory risk.

The appeal letter must directly rebut the payer's denial rationale with supporting evidence. This is not simple rule application (execution) or multi-system coordination (orchestration), but rather evidence synthesis requiring clinical judgment to construct a persuasive case.

Requires judgment in selecting which clinical evidence best supports medical necessity against payer-specific criteria, but the criteria themselves are published and structured. The fragmentation across 3-5 different systems per request — each payer with its own portal, form, criteria, and documentation requirements — is the primary orchestration challenge.

- Claim Denial Root Cause Analysis and Appeal Preparation :: Requires judgment to classify denial root causes, determine appealability, select appropriate appeal strategy, and synthesize clinical documentation into persuasive arguments against payer determinations
- Clean Claim Submission and Scrubbing :: Requires coordinating data from multiple systems—practice management system or EHR billing module, charge capture records, eligibility verification results, prior authorization records, claim scrubbing engine, clearinghouse, and payer portals—to produce clean claims that pass all edits on first submission. The scrubbing process involves cross-referencing coding against NCCI edits, LCD/NCD medical necessity rules, payer fee schedules, modifier requirements, timely filing deadlines, and patient demographic/insurance data accuracy. This multi-system orchestration with conditional logic branching distinguishes it from single-source execution tasks.
- Clinical Documentation Improvement (CDI) Query Response Review :: CDI specialist must adjudicate whether physician response adequately addresses the documentation gap, aligns with clinical indicators, and supports compliant code assignment
- Inpatient Medical Coding and DRG Assignment :: The coder must adjudicate between multiple possible code assignments by interpreting clinical documentation against ICD-10-CM Official Guidelines, ICD-10-PCS root operation definitions, AHA Coding Clinic advisories, and MS-DRG optimization rules. For each discharge the coder determines: (1) the principal diagnosis per UHDDS definition by weighing multiple documented conditions; (2) which secondary diagnoses meet reporting criteria and whether they function as CCs or MCCs; (3) the correct ICD-10-PCS root operation by mapping surgical terminology to PCS definitions (e.g., Excision vs. Resection vs. Extraction); (4) POA indicator assignment requiring clinical timeline reconstruction; (5) whether the resulting MS-DRG assignment is clinically coherent and optimized given documented conditions; and (6) whether clinical documentation supports the coded data or requires a physician query.
- Laboratory Prior Authorization Processing :: The specialist must orchestrate information across multiple systems and stakeholders: querying the laboratory information system (LIS) for test order details and specimen status, accessing payer portals to determine PA requirements and submit requests, gathering clinical documentation from ordering providers (often via fax or EHR portal), cross-referencing payer medical policies and clinical criteria databases (EviCore, Carelon/AIM, InterQual) against the patient's clinical indication, and coordinating with the ordering physician's office when additional documentation is needed. Unlike general medical PA where the provider's office initiates, laboratory PA is frequently initiated by the laboratory after receiving the test order, requiring outbound coordination with the ordering provider's office to obtain clinical documentation the lab does not possess. When requests are denied, the specialist must coordinate appeals involving laboratory medical directors and ordering physicians.
- Patient Insurance Eligibility Verification :: The specialist must orchestrate information across multiple systems: querying the practice management system or EHR for scheduled patient demographics and insurance information, submitting EDI 270 eligibility inquiries or checking payer portals for real-time eligibility responses (271), interpreting benefit details including coverage status, deductibles met, copay amounts, coinsurance percentages, and prior authorization requirements. For discrepancies or inactive coverage, the specialist must contact the patient to obtain updated insurance information, potentially re-verify with a different payer, and calculate estimated patient responsibility. When services require prior authorization, the specialist must flag and route to the authorization team. The workflow requires coordination across clearinghouse systems, multiple payer portals, the EHR/PMS, and direct patient communication.
- Transitional Care Management Documentation (TCM) :: TCM requires orchestrating multiple time-sensitive activities across a 30-day service period: receiving and processing hospital discharge notifications (ADT alerts), making interactive patient contact within 2 business days, obtaining and reviewing the hospital discharge summary, performing comprehensive medication reconciliation, coordinating follow-up appointments and referrals, managing non-face-to-face care coordination (test follow-up, patient education, community resource referral), conducting a face-to-face visit within the required timeframe (7 or 14 days), and documenting all components to support CPT 99495 or 99496 billing. The workflow spans multiple systems (EHR, hospital HIE/ADT feed, pharmacy records, patient communication tools) and involves handoffs between clinical staff roles.
- Patient Risk Stratification Assessment :: Risk stratification requires synthesizing heterogeneous data from multiple systems—clinical diagnoses from EHR problem lists, claims-based risk scores (HCC/RAF), utilization history (ED visits, hospitalizations, readmissions), pharmacy data (polypharmacy, high-risk medications), laboratory trends (HbA1c, eGFR, BNP), functional status assessments, behavioral health screens (PHQ-9, GAD-7), and social determinant indicators (housing instability, food insecurity, transportation barriers)—into a unified patient risk profile. The synthesis must reconcile conflicting signals (e.g., a patient with well-controlled HbA1c but frequent ED visits for hypoglycemia, or a patient with low clinical complexity but high social risk). Unlike pure adjudication (which applies rules to individual cases), risk stratification requires population-level pattern recognition and threshold calibration to ensure the stratification model produces actionable tier distributions that match available care management resources.
- Post-Acute Care Coordination Documentation :: Post-acute care coordination documentation requires orchestrating multiple parallel and sequential activities across the patient's entire post-acute stay: intake processing and clinical information reconciliation from the transferring hospital, initial comprehensive assessment (MDS for SNF, OASIS for home health), interdisciplinary care plan development with input from nursing, therapy, medical, dietary, social work, and pharmacy, ongoing utilization review and insurance authorization management, daily/weekly progress documentation updates, interdisciplinary team meetings with documentation of plan revisions, communication with the patient's primary care physician and specialists, family/caregiver education and coordination, discharge planning from the post-acute setting including home safety evaluation and community resource arrangement, and final transfer/discharge documentation assembly. The workflow spans multiple systems (post-acute EHR, hospital EHR access, insurance portals, pharmacy systems, community resource databases) and requires real-time adaptation as patient clinical status, therapy progress, insurance coverage, and discharge readiness evolve.
- Treatment Plan Documentation for Chronic Disease Management :: The clinician must orchestrate multiple data streams (encounter assessment, recent labs, imaging, pharmacy records, specialist notes, patient self- reports, SDOH screening) into a unified treatment plan that addresses each active chronic condition. The treatment plan requires cross-referencing medication lists for interactions and contraindications across conditions, reconciling potentially conflicting guideline recommendations (e.g., beta-blocker for heart failure vs. beta-agonist for COPD), setting individualized goals calibrated to disease severity and patient capacity, coordinating referrals and follow-up across multiple specialists, and documenting the plan in a structured EHR format that supports care continuity, quality reporting, and billing. Monthly or quarterly plan revisions require synthesizing interval changes (new lab results, ED visits, hospitalizations, medication changes, patient-reported symptom trajectories) and updating goals and interventions accordingly. The workflow spans clinical reasoning, patient communication, care team coordination, and regulatory documentation compliance.
- Ambulatory/Professional Fee Coding (E&M and CPT) :: The coder must adjudicate between multiple possible code assignments by interpreting clinical documentation against coding guidelines, AMA MDM tables, NCCI edits, and payer-specific rules. Requires judgment calls on E&M level selection (MDM vs. time-based), modifier applicability (25, 59, XE/XP/XS/XU), medical necessity linkage between diagnosis and procedure codes, and whether documentation supports the level of service billed. Not purely mechanical — coders regularly encounter ambiguous documentation requiring clinical interpretation and compliant provider queries.
- CDI Query Response Tracking and Follow-up Management :: This workflow is primarily orchestration rather than adjudication. The CDI specialist or manager must coordinate multiple concurrent query threads across a caseload of patients, managing time-based escalation triggers, routing follow-up actions to appropriate parties (verbal reminder, electronic re-notification, physician advisor referral, department chair notification), generating aggregate tracking reports, and ensuring no queries fall through the cracks before patient discharge. The complexity lies in managing the parallel tracking of dozens of open queries with different aging timelines, priority levels, and escalation statuses rather than making individual clinical judgments.
- Charge Capture and Reconciliation :: Requires coordinating data from multiple clinical and financial systems— EHR encounter records, operating room logs, pharmacy dispensing systems, radiology information systems, laboratory information systems, supply chain/implant tracking, and the hospital chargemaster—to ensure all billable services are captured and correctly coded. The reconciliation process involves cross-referencing appointment schedules against charge postings, identifying discrepancies, investigating root causes with clinical departments, and resolving exceptions before claims submission. This multi-system orchestration distinguishes it from single-source adjudication tasks.
- Claim Denial Appeal Documentation :: Requires synthesizing clinical documentation, coding data, payer policy language, and regulatory standards into a cohesive, persuasive appeal narrative that maps patient-specific clinical evidence to medical necessity criteria and contractual coverage provisions
- Clinical Documentation Improvement (CDI) Query Review :: Each chart review requires the CDI specialist to adjudicate whether the physician's clinical documentation accurately reflects the patient's severity of illness and resource utilization; the specialist must identify documentation gaps by cross-referencing clinical indicators (lab values, vitals, medications, procedures) against documented diagnoses, determine whether a compliant query is warranted, craft a non-leading query with appropriate clinical indicators, and evaluate the physician's response for clinical validity and coding impact
- Concurrent Inpatient Chart Review with Working DRG Assignment :: The CDI specialist must adjudicate between competing clinical evidence, reconcile documentation from multiple providers (attending, consulting, surgical), determine whether documented conditions meet clinical criteria and coding guidelines, and decide whether to query physicians for clarification versus accepting documentation as-is. Requires clinical judgment on severity of illness and risk of mortality implications.
- Discharge Planning Documentation :: Discharge planning documentation requires orchestrating multiple parallel and sequential activities across an interdisciplinary care team over the duration of a hospital stay: initial screening and risk stratification at admission, ongoing assessment of discharge needs as the clinical picture evolves, coordination with multiple specialty services and ancillary departments, insurance authorization for post-acute services, patient and family education and preference assessment, identification and evaluation of post-acute care facilities, arrangement of home health services and durable medical equipment, medication reconciliation coordination, and assembly of the complete discharge planning documentation package. The workflow spans multiple systems (EHR care management module, insurance portals, post-acute referral platforms, fax/phone communication) and requires real-time adaptation as patient clinical status, insurance approvals, and bed availability change.
- HEDIS Medical Record Chart Abstraction and Review :: Primarily rule-based execution against NCQA technical specifications. Abstractors apply defined criteria to determine numerator compliance, with limited judgment required for ambiguous documentation. Quality assurance layer adds adjudication for discrepant findings.
- ICD-10-CM/PCS Diagnosis and Procedure Coding :: The coder must adjudicate between multiple possible code assignments by interpreting clinical documentation against ICD-10-CM Official Guidelines, ICD-10-PCS root operation definitions, AHA Coding Clinic advisories, and MS-DRG optimization rules. For procedure coding, the coder constructs seven-character ICD-10-PCS codes by determining the correct section, body system, root operation, body part, approach, device, and qualifier from operative reports that use varied surgical terminology. Requires judgment on principal diagnosis sequencing, CC/MCC capture, present on admission (POA) indicator assignment, and whether documentation supports querying the physician for clarification.
- Medical Transcription and Clinical Documentation Completion :: Clinical documentation completion requires synthesizing raw audio or dictated speech into structured clinical notes that conform to specialty-specific templates, documentation standards (SOAP, H&P, procedure note formats), and coding requirements. The system must disambiguate medical homophones (e.g., ileum vs. ilium), resolve speaker diarization in multi-party encounters, map clinical concepts to appropriate note sections, infer structured data elements (medications, dosages, diagnoses) from unstructured conversation, and produce notes that align with the clinician's documentation style and specialty conventions. Beyond mechanical transcription, the synthesis involves contextualizing clinical conversation against patient history, lab results, and medication lists from the EHR to produce a coherent clinical narrative.
- Nursing Shift Assessment Documentation :: Each nursing shift assessment requires synthesizing information from multiple sources — direct patient observation and physical examination across 6-12 body systems, vital signs and telemetry data, medication administration records, prior shift documentation, physician orders, laboratory results, and patient/family verbal reports. The nurse must integrate real-time bedside findings with pre-existing EHR data to identify changes from baseline, recognize clinical deterioration patterns, and generate a structured documentation record across flowsheet categories. While individual body system assessments follow established patterns (normal vs. abnormal findings), the synthesis across a patient's evolving clinical picture — particularly identifying which changes are clinically significant and require notification or intervention — requires professional judgment beyond rule-based execution.

- Patient Safety Event Investigation and Root Cause Analysis :: Requires structured judgment to determine whether a patient safety event resulted from individual human error, systemic process failure, communication breakdown, or a combination of contributing factors; investigator must evaluate competing causal hypotheses using structured RCA methodology, weigh clinical evidence and system data, and reach defensible root cause conclusions that withstand accreditation and regulatory scrutiny while maintaining a just culture approach that distinguishes system failures from individual accountability
- Pharmacy Insurance Claim Rejection Resolution :: Requires real-time orchestration across pharmacy management system, PBM adjudication responses, prescriber communication, payer help desks, and electronic prior authorization platforms. The technician must interpret reject codes, determine the appropriate resolution path (data correction, therapeutic alternative, prior authorization, override request, or plan change), execute the fix, and resubmit — often within minutes while the patient waits. Multiple rejections on a single claim require sequential troubleshooting.
- Pharmacy Prior Authorization Request Preparation and Submission :: The specialist must orchestrate information across multiple systems and stakeholders: receiving the PA trigger from the pharmacy (claim rejection at POS or retrospective review), querying the EHR or prescriber for clinical documentation, checking PBM formulary and coverage criteria, determining the specific PA requirements (step therapy, quantity limit exception, non-formulary exception, or clinical PA), gathering supporting clinical evidence (diagnosis, lab results, prior medication trials with dates and outcomes), completing the payer-specific or CoverMyMeds PA form, and submitting via ePA, fax, or payer portal. For specialty medications, complexity increases with requirements for clinical lab values, prior biologic trials, and specialist attestation.
- Progress Note Daily Rounding Documentation :: Each daily progress note requires synthesizing multiple data streams generated over the preceding 24 hours — overnight nursing assessments, vital sign trends, new laboratory and imaging results, medication administration records, interval patient symptoms, physical examination findings, and input from consulting services — into a structured clinical document that captures current clinical status, updated assessment, and revised plan of care. The clinician must integrate objective data with subjective patient reports and apply clinical judgment to determine which findings are clinically significant, how they affect the differential diagnosis, and what management changes are warranted. This is multi-source synthesis performed under time pressure during or immediately after bedside rounds.
- Specialist Referral Documentation :: The coordinator must orchestrate information across multiple systems and stakeholders: extracting relevant clinical data from the EHR (progress notes, lab results, imaging, medication lists), verifying insurance eligibility and referral authorization requirements, selecting the appropriate specialist based on clinical need and network status, assembling a complete referral package with supporting documentation, transmitting the package to the specialist office, and tracking the referral through to specialist visit completion and report return (closing the loop). - Specialty Pharmacy Patient Enrollment and Benefits Investigation :: The coordinator must orchestrate information and actions across multiple systems and stakeholders: receiving the referral or e-prescription from the prescriber, verifying patient demographics and insurance information, determining whether the medication is covered under the medical benefit (J-code) or pharmacy benefit (NDC), contacting payers to verify eligibility and coverage, identifying prior authorization and step therapy requirements, initiating PA requests, enrolling patients in manufacturer hub programs and copay/PAP assistance, coordinating with prescriber offices for clinical documentation, and communicating coverage and cost information back to the patient. The workflow requires simultaneous navigation of 4-6 different systems per case with multi-party coordination between patient, prescriber, payer, and manufacturer.

========== P1 WORKFLOWS (70) ==========
- Accounts Receivable Follow-up and Collections :: Requires coordinating across multiple payer portals, billing systems, patient records, and communication channels to resolve unpaid balances. Each account requires sequencing status checks, identifying root causes of non-payment, initiating corrective actions, and routing to appropriate escalation paths (rebill, appeal, payment plan, or write-off)
- Care Coordination Referral Tracking and Closure :: Requires orchestrating information across multiple systems (EHR, referral platforms, payer portals, fax/phone), coordinating between referring providers, specialists, and patients, and managing multi-step follow-up sequences until closure is achieved.
- CDI-Coding DRG Reconciliation Review :: The DRG reconciliation reviewer must adjudicate between two competing DRG assignments: the CDI specialist's working DRG and the coder's final DRG. This requires evaluating which principal diagnosis selection is correct per ICD-10 Official Guidelines Section II, whether CC/MCC designations are properly supported by documentation, whether present-on-admission indicators are accurate, and whether coding guideline interpretations are correctly applied. When consensus cannot be reached, the reviewer may escalate to a physician advisor for clinical determination. This is classic adjudication between two expert opinions with documented rationale.
- CMS Star Ratings Quality Measure Preparation and Submission :: The quality team must orchestrate data collection across multiple systems and data sources (claims warehouse, EHR, pharmacy benefit, immunization registries, survey vendors, supplemental data feeds), apply complex HEDIS measure logic with denominator/numerator specifications and exclusion criteria, manage medical record retrieval and hybrid measure abstraction, coordinate with CMS-approved CAHPS and HOS survey vendors, validate data against Patient-Level Detail (PLD) file requirements, reconcile discrepancies between summary-level and patient-level data, and submit finalized results through NCQA's Interactive Data Submission System (IDSS) and CMS's Health Plan Management System (HPMS). Requires judgment in applying measure specifications to edge cases, resolving data quality issues, and prioritizing improvement interventions across 45 measures to optimize overall Star Rating.
- Corrective Action Plan (CAP) Development and Tracking :: Requires synthesizing multiple inputs — survey findings, clinical policies, staff interviews, resident records, and regulatory requirements — into a cohesive corrective action plan that addresses root causes while satisfying the five required CMS elements for each cited deficiency.
- HEDIS Quality Measure Abstraction and Reporting :: Primarily rule-based execution against detailed NCQA HEDIS technical specifications. Abstractors apply explicit criteria to medical records to determine numerator compliance. Limited judgment is required for ambiguous clinical documentation, but the specifications themselves are prescriptive. The overread and IRR processes add a layer of quality adjudication.
- HEDIS Quality Measure Reporting and Gap Analysis :: Coordinates multiple data streams (claims, pharmacy, lab, EMR, supplemental data), manages chart chase logistics across hundreds of providers, sequences abstraction/analysis/reporting phases, and synthesizes performance across 90+ measures with regulatory deadlines
- Laboratory Claims Denial Analysis and Appeals :: Requires judgment to interpret denial reason codes in the context of laboratory-specific coverage policies (LCDs/NCDs), determine whether denial stems from coding error, missing documentation, medical necessity failure, or CLIA compliance issue, and select the appropriate corrective action or appeal strategy
- Medication Therapy Management (MTM) Comprehensive Medication Review :: The pharmacist must adjudicate multiple competing clinical considerations—drug interactions, therapeutic duplication, adherence barriers, cost optimization, formulary alignment—and synthesize a prioritized action plan. Each medication must be evaluated against the patient's complete clinical picture.
- Provider Credentialing and Privileging Verification :: The credentialing specialist must orchestrate verification requests across 10-15 distinct primary sources (state licensing boards, NPDB, ABMS, AMA Physician Masterfile, medical schools, training programs, DEA, OIG LEIE, SAM.gov, malpractice carriers, peer references, hospital affiliations), track response timelines against NCQA 120-day windows, reconcile discrepancies across sources, and assemble a complete credentials file for committee review. The complexity lies in parallel coordination of many independent verification threads, managing exceptions and discrepancies, and ensuring all elements are current and complete before the file advances to the credentials committee. Judgment is required for discrepancy resolution but the individual verification steps follow standardized procedures.
- Utilization Review Concurrent Stay Documentation :: Each concurrent review episode requires the UR nurse to adjudicate whether a patient's continued inpatient stay meets medical necessity criteria by weighing clinical documentation against evidence-based criteria (InterQual or MCG). The nurse must determine: (1) Does the patient's current clinical status justify the current level of care? (2) Is the patient a responder, partial responder, or nonresponder to treatment per the criteria tool? (3) Should the patient status remain inpatient, be converted to observation, or be discharged/transferred to a lower level of care? (4) Is the clinical documentation sufficient to support the medical necessity determination? When criteria are not clearly met, the UR nurse must escalate to the physician advisor for a second-level clinical review, and potentially initiate a peer-to-peer discussion with the payer's medical director. This is fundamentally an adjudication workflow — applying clinical criteria to individual patient facts to render a coverage determination, with escalation pathways for ambiguous cases.
- Acute Care Discharge Planning :: Requires coordinating multiple stakeholders, systems, or sequential processes toward a defined outcome — for Acute Care Discharge Planning, this means managing handoffs, timelines, and information flows across parties so the workflow reaches completion with all dependencies satisfied.
- Care Planning :: Requires integrating information from multiple disparate sources into a coherent, unified output — for Care Planning, this means transforming raw inputs (records, data, precedents) into a structured document that communicates findings, positions, or recommendations.
- CDI Database Entry and Case Documentation :: The database entry and case documentation workflow is primarily execution-level: populating predefined fields in CDI tracking platforms, transcribing review findings from the chart review into structured database records, logging query statuses, and updating case dispositions. While the upstream clinical review requires adjudication-level judgment, the data entry step itself follows standardized templates and field definitions with limited discretionary decision-making. The specialist must accurately translate clinical findings into database fields but does not make new clinical judgments during the documentation step.
- Critical Value Notification and Documentation :: Largely rule-based execution with structured escalation paths. The core notification follows a deterministic protocol, though judgment is needed for specimen validity assessment (e.g., hemolyzed potassium, prolonged glucose specimens) and identifying the correct responsible provider for notification.
- DRG Validation and Clinical Coding Audit :: Each audited case requires multi-layered adjudication. The auditor must: (1) independently determine the correct principal diagnosis by applying UHDDS definition and Official Coding Guidelines Section II against the complete medical record; (2) adjudicate CC/MCC status of each secondary diagnosis against clinical documentation support and the CMS CC/MCC Exclusion List; (3) validate POA indicators by tracing the clinical timeline from ED/admission through discharge; (4) verify ICD-10-PCS procedure codes against operative reports using root operation definitions; (5) run the MS-DRG grouper with auditor-determined codes and compare against the original DRG assignment; and (6) classify each discrepancy by root cause (documentation deficiency, coding error, sequencing error, clinical validation failure). When the auditor's DRG differs from the original, the auditor must document the specific coding guideline or clinical rationale supporting the change. Cases involving sepsis sequencing, malnutrition severity, mechanical ventilation hours, and heart failure classification require particularly nuanced clinical-coding judgment that goes beyond rule application.
- Electronic Prior Authorization Submission (Portal/EHR) :: Requires performing a defined, repeatable sequence of steps following established protocols with minimal independent judgment — for Electronic Prior Authorization Submission (Portal/EHR), this means completing structured tasks accurately and efficiently within a predefined system or process.
