**Brainstorm: CF Hyperammonemic Decompensation and Recovery**

Concept pitch for Team Lead review. World type: typical clinical (inpatient). Snapshot: discharge to acute rehab, Feb 20, 2026\.

**Patient:** Eric Brenick, DOB 04/18/1999 (age 26), MRN ZZZ-0042170 (synthetic).

# **1\. World setup**

Eric Brenick is a 26-year-old man with cystic fibrosis whose physiology runs along a single gut-liver-brain ammonia axis: biliary cirrhosis with portal hypertension (TIPS placed 11/25), recurrent distal intestinal obstruction syndrome (DIOS), CF-related diabetes, pancreatic insufficiency with malnutrition and sarcopenia, and a seizure disorder. Admitted Jan 11, 2026 in hyperammonemic crisis (ammonia \>400) from a DIOS-driven enteric nitrogen surge into a body with no ammonia-clearance reserve, producing cerebral edema with impending herniation. Over a floor-ICU-floor course he recovers (ammonia normalizes, brainstem reflexes return on dated serial exams, mental status clears) and is discharged to acute rehab Feb 20, 2026\. The admission is the shared substrate; every task is an independent encounter at or after discharge. Task 1 (discharge summary) is the integration anchor.

# **2\. Major friction points**

## **Central**

* **Transition-of-care reconciliation (inpatient vs outpatient regimen).** Discharging hospitalist wants a clean rehab handoff and under-specifies the conditional Trikafta resumption; outpatient CF/hepatology expects the disease-modifying regimen restored promptly; rehab follows the written orders literally.

* **Transplant listing adjudication (transplant team vs selection committee).** Transplant hepatologist/surgeon advocates listing now given youth and reversible neurologic injury; selection committee / neuropsychology raise long-term graft survival and the bifrontal injury, advocating deferral. Resolves to LIST.

## **Secondary (anchored to notes a task draws on)**

* **Neurocritical care vs hepatology/transplant during the ICU course:** ICP-control measures (PEEP limits, sustained sedation, hypertonic saline) versus transplant timing, when neuro status is both the threat and the gate. Anchored in the ICU progress notes and transplant-eval documentation; Task 8 (listing eval) draws on it.

* **Family vs team goals-of-care during the herniation scare:** continued aggressive support versus comfort, before the recovery. Anchored in the family-meeting note that Task 1 (discharge summary) references in the hospital course.
